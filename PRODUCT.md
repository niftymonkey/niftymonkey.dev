# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Peer engineers first: experienced software engineers, tech leads, staff engineers, and engineering managers working through AI-assisted development without the hype. They are intelligent, busy, and skeptical. They have read a lot of confident writing that turned out to be wrong, and they trust craft over polish.

Second: hiring managers and recruiters deciding whether to start a conversation. They should get the point of any surface in two minutes without the page having been written for them.

Context: reading on a laptop or phone, often on a break, often sent here by a colleague. Nobody is in a workflow. They are deciding whether the author is worth learning from.

## Product Purpose

niftymonkey.dev is the author's personal site. It is not a personal brand, a content machine, or a growth engine. It exists to make useful engineering thinking easier to learn from, and to show the software behind that thinking.

Three surfaces:

- The homepage lists projects as a terminal directory listing. It is a scan, and that is all it needs to be.
- The notebook holds engineering writing worth keeping: a lab notebook, never a blog. Entries carry evidence, get re-reviewed, and are superseded rather than deleted.
- The showcase (in progress) is one page that gives a real, connected view of what the author built with AI over a year: fifteen working projects, with pictures and the hard parts named. The reader should leave believing one thing above all: he ships real things with AI. Volume and variety, not demos.

Success is not traffic. Success is someone saying "this helped me think more clearly," or a reader who came to scan and stayed to understand.

## Brand Personality

Three words: calm, curious, hand-made.

The voice is a person talking, never an instrument reporting. Evidence-based, practical, respectful of complexity. The author does not present himself as someone with all the answers; he shares what he tried, what surprised him, and what changed his mind.

For the showcase specifically: it should feel like someone showing you their workshop. Warm and plain. "Here is what I made this year, and here is why it was hard." No sales voice. The pictures are the work itself, and the words say what was hard about it.

Emotional goals: trust, recognition ("this person builds the way I want to"), and the occasional small delight found in precision rather than effects.

## Anti-references

- Startup case study pages: problem, solution, results, metric callouts, testimonials.
- AI hype posts: "I built 15 apps with AI!", counter animations, sparkle icons, tweet-thread tone.
- GitHub profile READMEs: badge rows, pinned repo cards, stats widgets.
- Anything metrics-driven or attention-grabbing: animated statistics, fade-in on scroll, hover parallax, ambient motion.
- Anything recognizably of its year. If a choice is visibly 2026, it is wrong.
- Share buttons, reactions, view counts, email capture, subscribe modals, comment sections.

## Design Principles

1. **Readability, clarity, longevity, accessibility, focus on content.** In that order. Every trade-off resolves down this list.
2. **The work is the proof.** Show the real thing: the actual screen, the actual decision record, the actual hard part. Never a mockup on a tilted phone, never a claim without its evidence.
3. **A person talking, never a brochure.** Copy is written the way the author would say it out loud to a colleague. No superlatives, no hype, no absolutes.
4. **Quietly crafted, not decorated.** Delight lives in the precision and the voice. If it draws attention to the container instead of the contents, cut it.
5. **Timeless over fashionable.** Choose what will still read well in ten years. Nothing to rot, nothing to date.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Contrast of at least 4.5:1 on text, keyboard access to everything, visible focus, prefers-reduced-motion respected, and alt text on every screenshot that says what the screen shows, not what the project is. Light and dark are first-class peers; neither is the fallback.

## Design Read

For the taste skill, in its own vocabulary: minimalist, terminal-native, trust-first, editorial-quiet. Low motion, moderate density, low variance from the existing site.
