Boswell

A self-hosted, voice-first personal AI that keeps a continuously distilled model of one person's life, on hardware they control, and surfaces things at the moment they matter.

Plate 1 · the chat surface, citation cards in the margin

What was hard

  1. Deciding who a name refers to. The tiered resolver was torn out and rebuilt as a single pure function, and the substrate decision was written down instead of adopting a graph database.
  2. Making the memory admit what it does not know. Grounding, candor on failure, contradiction crosscheck, and fact supersession each needed their own decision record.
  3. Getting voice to work end to end. WebRTC through Docker NAT, a relay, barge-in, and client-owned turn ends took 27 commits and four research notes.

Brief

Long videos hide whether they are worth watching behind the watching. Brief turns a YouTube link into a timestamped, structured summary, and keeps every brief in a searchable library.

Plate 7 · brief detail, player and chapters
Plate 8 · the library

What was hard

  1. Adding video frames meant downloading video bytes, which YouTube's terms name directly. The answer was to move that work to the user's machine.
  2. That pivot turned a standalone CLI into an authenticated thin client: device flow, token refresh, and a server-side LLM gateway with a usage ledger.
  3. Collections came in as a full vertical slice: schema, API, UI, per-clip summaries, public share pages, and continuous playback.
Plate 9 · a public collection page
